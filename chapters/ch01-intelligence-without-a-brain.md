## Chapter Plan

**Core claim of the chapter:** Intelligence is not something brains invented. Brains scaled up a trick that life was already running. Once you see that, "smart" stops being a yes/no property of things with heads and becomes a measurable dial that applies to cells, tissues, colonies, robots, and software agents alike.

### Sections to write

1. **Three things that shouldn't work** — Opening triptych: the chemotactic bacterium, the wound that closes in the correct shape, *Physarum polycephalum* solving a maze. Each described concretely, each with no nervous system anywhere in the story.
2. **What we mean by "smart"** — William James' definition of intelligence: same end, different means. Not "does it have neurons" but "does it reach a goal flexibly when you block the obvious path." Introduce the test the rest of the book uses.
3. **The brain chauvinism problem** — Why we default to assuming cognition requires neurons, where that assumption came from historically, and the specific predictions it gets wrong.
4. **Enter TAME** — Michael Levin's Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere, introduced as a *framework for engineers*, not a metaphysics. Preview the six ideas that structure Chapters 2–7.
5. **Why this lands now** — 2026 context: we are surrounded by systems whose competence we did not design in detail. Agentic AI, self-organizing infrastructure, multi-agent swarms. We need vocabulary for goal-directedness without assuming human-style minds.
6. **The honest limits** — Where basal cognition is speculative, what critics say (the "just chemistry" objection, the deflationary reading), and how to hold the framework productively without overclaiming.
7. **How to read this book** — Chapters 2–7 are the six ideas. Chapter 8 is the experiment: a real, cheap, runnable test of whether AI agents habituate the way living things do.
