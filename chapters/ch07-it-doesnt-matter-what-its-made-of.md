# It Doesn't Matter What It's Made Of

## Chapter Plan

**Core claim of the chapter:** The organizational principles behind basal cognition are substrate-independent. They are claims about relationships between parts, not about the chemistry of the parts. This is what makes the framework applicable to machines — and what makes the ethics urgent.

### Sections to write

1. **Xenobots** — Frog embryonic skin cells, dissociated and left alone. They do not make skin. They self-assemble into motile, self-repairing organisms that navigate, aggregate debris, and have no ancestors. Describe carefully, including what they can and cannot do.
2. **Anthrobots** — The same result from adult human tracheal cells. Why this matters more than xenobots: not embryonic, not amphibian, and demonstrably capable of assisting neuronal repair in vitro.
3. **What the genome was and wasn't doing** — Neither creature is genetically modified. The genome permitted a form evolution never selected for. Latent morphospace: the set of bodies your cells could build but never do.
4. **Substrate independence, stated precisely** — Distinguish the strong claim (any medium can host cognition) from the weak claim (the *principles* generalize). Defend the weak claim; flag the strong one as open.
5. **Functionalism and its critics** — The honest philosophy section. Multiple realizability, the standard objections, and what basal cognition adds empirically rather than argumentatively.
6. **Synthetic and hybrid systems** — Robots with regenerative goal structures, bioelectric-inspired control, self-organizing swarms. What has actually been built by 2026 versus what is proposed.
7. **Software as a substrate** — The transfer to AI. Where the mapping is real: goal-directedness, error correction, multi-scale composition, setpoints, coupling. Where it breaks: no metabolism, no genuine physical stakes, discrete rather than continuous state. Be rigorous about the disanalogies.
8. **The ethics we are not ready for** — If cognition is a continuum and substrate does not matter, moral consideration cannot be gated on being made of meat or having neurons. Practical framing for engineers who now build things that might sit somewhere on the scale.

### Concepts requiring the 5-step plain-language treatment

- Latent morphospace
- Multiple realizability and its limits
