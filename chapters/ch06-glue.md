## Chapter Plan

**Core claim of the chapter:** Individuality is not given, it is manufactured — and the manufacturing process is information sharing. Selfhood scales with communication bandwidth. Cut the channel and one agent becomes two; open the channel and many become one.

### Sections to write

1. **The boundary question** — Why is *you* the boundary? Not the cell, not the family, not the colony. Something makes this particular collection cohere into one goal-pursuing unit. Take the question seriously before answering it.
2. **Gap junctions: the physical glue** — Direct cytoplasmic channels between cells. What passes through: ions, small molecules, voltage. Why sharing state at this level makes separate memories indistinguishable.
3. **From memory-sharing to selfhood** — Levin's argument that a shared, non-attributable memory pool *is* the mechanism of binding. If neither cell can tell whose signal it was, the boundary between them stops being functional.
4. **Cutting the glue** — Sever gap junction coupling and a single regenerative individual becomes two. Direct experimental evidence. The self is a variable, not a constant.
5. **The bandwidth ladder** — Individuality as a function of coupling strength. Loosely coupled colony → tightly coupled organism. Slime mold and bee colonies as intermediate cases. Where the transition happens and why it can be gradual.
6. **Failure modes of glue** — Too little: fragmentation, defection, cancer (callback to Chapter 3). Too much: loss of local autonomy, single point of failure, groupthink at the cellular level. There is an optimum and it is contextual.
7. **Multi-agent AI as a coupling problem** — Shared context, shared scratchpads, shared memory stores as engineered gap junctions. Why a team of agents with a shared state file behaves like one agent, and a team without it behaves like competitors. The 2026 agent-team pattern read as bandwidth engineering.
8. **Designing the right amount of coupling** — Practical guidance with a worked case: choosing between isolated sub-agents, shared-memory teams, and full state-fusion.

### Concepts requiring the 5-step plain-language treatment

- Individuality as a function of bandwidth
- The coupling optimum (too little / too much)
