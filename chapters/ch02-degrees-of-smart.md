## Chapter Plan

**Core claim of the chapter:** Agency is a continuum, and where a system sits on that continuum determines the *kind of intervention that works on it*. This is the first TAME idea and the most immediately practical one.

### Sections to write

1. **The clock, the dog, and the person** — Three systems, three completely different repair strategies. Build the intuition before naming anything.
2. **The persuadability spectrum** — Formal introduction. From "rewire the mechanism" to "provide a signal and let the system figure out the rest." Position thermostats, servomotors, single cells, trained animals, humans.
3. **Why the wrong lever fails** — Hardware fixes applied to persuadable systems (over-specification, brittleness). Persuasion applied to non-persuadable systems (magical thinking). Worked examples from both directions.
4. **The cell as a trainable system** — The pivot case. Sometimes you edit DNA. Sometimes you send one bioelectric signal and the cell builds an eye. Same organism, radically different intervention depth. Why the second is cheaper and more robust.
5. **Regenerative medicine as a control problem** — Reframing therapy: not "specify the outcome" but "give the tissue a goal and let it solve." Concrete current examples.
6. **The same spectrum for AI systems** — Fine-tuning vs. prompting vs. tool-provisioning vs. goal-setting, mapped onto the persuadability spectrum. Why prompt engineering *is* the persuasion end and weight surgery *is* the hardware end.
7. **Choosing the intervention level: a decision procedure** — A usable heuristic with a worked example on an agentic workflow.
